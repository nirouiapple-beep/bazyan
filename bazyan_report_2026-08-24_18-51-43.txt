## Market changes identified

- **Agriculture and irrigation strengthened:** Iraq announced contracts covering approximately 3.5 million dunams with modern irrigation systems, while officially acknowledging drought, declining rainfall, and weak sorting, packaging, labeling, and export systems. ([ina.iq](https://ina.iq/en/international/50465-moa-international-contracts-to-cover-35-million-dunams-with-modern-irrigation-systems.html?utm_source=openai))
- **Water infrastructure strengthened:** The Ministry of Water Resources is planning canal lining and transition from open channels to pipelines, with projects linked to the 2026 budget. ([ina.iq](https://ina.iq/ar/local/255421-.html?utm_source=openai))
- **Logistics strengthened:** TIR use became mandatory for transit by road in November 2025, supporting more formal cross-border logistics. ([iru.org](https://www.iru.org/news-resources/newsroom/secure-efficient-resilient-one-year-tir-iraq?utm_source=openai))
- **Housing remains large but execution-heavy:** The National Investment Commission continues promoting approximately one million housing units across Iraq. ([investpromo.gov.iq](https://investpromo.gov.iq/housing-program/?utm_source=openai))
- **Kurdistan electricity opportunity changed:** Runaki targets 24-hour electricity throughout the Kurdistan Region by the end of 2026, weakening the case for ordinary generator-based businesses but strengthening energy-efficiency, electrical contracting, and backup-power niches. ([runaki.gov.krd](https://runaki.gov.krd/en/?utm_source=openai))
- **Healthcare supply gaps remain material:** WHO reports shortages and logistical disruptions affecting medical supplies in several governorates, while Iraqi primary-care facilities face substantial medicine, supply, and service gaps. ([emro.who.int](https://www.emro.who.int/images/stories/eha/ME-Sitrep-2-19-March-26.pdf?utm_source=openai))
- **Digital payments continue formalization:** The Central Bank has issued expanding rules for electronic wallets, payment providers, cybersecurity, and digital banks. ([cbi.iq](https://www.cbi.iq/page/133?utm_source=openai))

## 1. Water-efficient irrigation and farm services

**Problem and demand:** Water scarcity, drought, declining rainfall, and inefficient open-channel irrigation are officially recognized problems. Government plans and contracts are moving toward modern irrigation systems. ([ina.iq](https://ina.iq/en/international/50465-moa-international-contracts-to-cover-35-million-dunams-with-modern-irrigation-systems.html?utm_source=openai))

**Solution:** Sell and install drip irrigation, pumps, filters, fertigation equipment, groundwater systems, maintenance, and agronomic monitoring.

- **Customers:** Commercial farms, cooperatives, greenhouse operators, municipalities, NGOs, agricultural contractors.
- **Why now:** Strong policy support and urgent water constraints.
- **Competitors:** International irrigation suppliers, agricultural-equipment dealers, local pump installers.
- **Competition:** Medium.
- **Advantage:** Local installation, maintenance, financing, and bundled farm-service contracts.
- **Revenue:** Equipment margin, installation fees, annual maintenance, crop-service subscriptions.
- **Startup estimate:** **$40,000–$180,000**.
- **Revenue potential:** $250,000–$1.5 million annually after expansion.
- **Profit potential:** Estimated net margin 10–20%.
- **Break-even:** 12–24 months.
- **First customers:** Farms near Kut, Hillah, Baqubah, Najaf, and Erbil; agricultural cooperatives.
- **Risks:** Procurement delays, water-rights issues, farmer credit risk, imported-equipment currency exposure.
- **Regulation:** Company registration, import/customs compliance, agricultural and water approvals where applicable.
- **Scalability:** High across central and northern Iraq.

## 2. Produce sorting, packaging, cold storage, and aggregation

**Problem and demand:** Iraq’s Agriculture Ministry specifically identifies weak sorting, packaging, labeling, marketing, and export processes. ([ina.iq](https://ina.iq/en/international/50465-moa-international-contracts-to-cover-35-million-dunams-with-modern-irrigation-systems.html?utm_source=openai))

**Solution:** Operate modular packhouses with grading, washing, labeling, palletization, refrigerated storage, and wholesale distribution.

- **Customers:** Farmers, supermarkets, restaurants, exporters, institutional buyers.
- **Best locations:** Wasit, Babil, Diyala, Erbil, Baghdad, Basra.
- **Competitors:** Existing wholesalers, informal market aggregators, supermarket distribution centers.
- **Competition:** Medium.
- **Advantage:** Reliable quality, traceability, reduced spoilage, scheduled collection.
- **Startup estimate:** $120,000–$600,000.
- **Revenue potential:** $500,000–$3 million annually.
- **Profit potential:** 8–18% net margin.
- **Break-even:** 18–36 months.
- **First customers:** Tomato, cucumber, potato, date, citrus, and poultry producers.
- **Risks:** Seasonal supply, electricity, cold-chain losses, customer concentration.
- **Regulation:** Food-safety, municipal, transport, warehouse, and possibly export approvals.
- **Scalability:** High if replicated near production zones.

## 3. Medical supplies and diagnostic-kit distribution

- **Competitors:** Established Iraqi distributors and multinational agents.

- **Scalability:** High, but compliance-heavy.

# Best 3 opportunities

## 1. Water-efficient irrigation and farm services — Score 82

Best combination of urgent need, public-policy support, repeat revenue, and moderate startup cost. It can begin as a distributor/installer without building a factory.

## 2. Produce aggregation, packaging, and cold chain — Score 80

Strongly supported by the government’s own statement that sorting, packaging, labeling, and export systems are weak. It can start with rented cold storage and outsourced transport.

## 3. Medical supplies and diagnostic distribution — Score 78

Demand is real and immediate, but licensing, working capital, tender risk, and established competitors make execution more difficult.

# Opportunity to validate first

## Recommended validation: irrigation plus farm-service brokerage

Do **not** begin by importing a full container or building a facility.

### Low-cost 21-day test

Budget: **$2,000–$7,000**

1. Select one corridor: Wasit–Babil or Diyala.
2. Interview 30 farmers and 5 agricultural cooperatives.
3. Identify three crops with high water or spoilage problems.
4. Obtain quotations from three irrigation suppliers.
5. Build a simple package: site survey, drip system, installation, maintenance.
6. Offer a paid farm audit for a small fixed fee.
7. Secure written expressions of interest from at least 10 farmers.
8. Obtain deposits from at least three customers.
9. Install one small demonstration plot.
10. Measure water use, yield, labor, and crop quality against a conventional plot.
11. Document results in Arabic and Kurdish.
12. Approach agricultural associations and local agronomists.
13. Test whether customers accept installment payments.
14. Calculate landed equipment cost and gross margin.
15. Select one local installer and one backup supplier.
16. Check import, agricultural, municipal, and tax requirements.
17. Offer maintenance contracts.
18. Test a crop-aggregation add-on for produce packaging.
19. Reject the model if fewer than three customers pay deposits.
20. Expand only after achieving a target gross margin above 25%.
21. Reassess after the first harvest cycle.

# Monitoring conclusion

### New or materially strengthened

- Modern irrigation and water-efficiency services.
- Produce grading, packaging, and cold-chain aggregation.
- Formal Development Road logistics and customs support.
- Medical-supply distribution, particularly diagnostic and emergency consumables.
- Water treatment in southern governorates.

### Stronger but more competitive

- Affordable construction materials.
- Food processing and import substitution.
- Digital-payment software for SMEs.

### Weaker than previously assumed

- Ordinary generator sales and basic household solar in Kurdistan, because Runaki is targeting region-wide 24-hour electricity by the end of 2026. ([runaki.gov.krd](https://runaki.gov.krd/en/?utm_source=openai))
- Large speculative housing development for small investors: demand is enormous, but financing, land, approvals, infrastructure, and payment collection make it unsuitable as a first low-cost venture.

### Opportunities requiring caution

- Large hospitals, desalination plants, housing cities, and industrial zones: genuine demand exists, but they require substantial capital, government contracting, and long execution periods.
- Public-sector supply contracts: attractive volume, but payment delays and tender compliance can overwhelm small firms.
